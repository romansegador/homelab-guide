Grafana and ArgoCD have users and rights of their own. Instead of a gate in front, they get a
**"Sign in with Authentik"** button. Authentik tells them who you are and which groups you're in,
and members of `admins` become administrators in each app.

<Aside type="tip" title="Do it with an AI agent">
The `single-sign-on` skill covers this page. See [Use with an AI agent](/start-here/agents/).
</Aside>

**Tested with:** Authentik 2026.8.5, Grafana 13.3 (kube-prometheus-stack 93.3.0), ArgoCD 3.6.1
(chart 10.12.1).

## A few ideas first

### OpenID Connect in one paragraph

Each app is registered in Authentik as an **OIDC client**, with an id (`grafana`, `argocd`), a
**client secret**, and the exact address Authentik may send you back to (the **redirect URI**). When
you click the button, the app sends you to Authentik; you log in there (with your second factor);
Authentik sends you back with a code; the app swaps that code, plus its client secret, for a signed
token that says who you are and lists your groups.

### One secret, read by both sides

The client secret must be the same in Authentik and in the app. You create it **once** in the
secrets manager, and both sides read that one entry: Authentik through an environment variable
that the blueprint reads with `!Env`, the app through its own ExternalSecret. Nothing is copied by
hand, and the value never appears in git.

### The local admin stays

Each app keeps its own administrator: Grafana's `admin` user (chapter 10), ArgoCD's `admin`
(chapter 3). If Authentik is down, that's your way in.

The reasoning behind each choice is in the [FAQ](/start-here/faq/#chapter-11--single-sign-on).

## Before you start

- [ ] [A login provider](/single-sign-on/identity-provider/): you log in to Authentik.
- [ ] Your user in Authentik is in the `admins` group.

## Set it up

<Steps>

1. **Create the two client secrets.** In **Bitwarden Secrets Manager**, in the cluster's project,
   add two entries, each with a random value of letters and digits:
   - **`authentik-oidc-grafana`**
   - **`authentik-oidc-argocd`**

   ```sh title="Laptop"
   openssl rand -base64 48 | tr -d '/+=' | head -c 64; echo
   ```

   Letters and digits only, so the value never needs escaping in a URL.

2. **Let Authentik read them.** From
   [`examples/11-single-sign-on`](https://github.com/romansegador/homelab-guide/tree/main/examples/11-single-sign-on):
   - `manifests/authentik/secrets.yaml`: add the second document, `authentik-oidc`, with its
     `grafana` and `argocd` keys (leave out `proxmox` until page 4).
   - `apps/authentik.yaml`: add `AK_OIDC_GRAFANA_SECRET` and `AK_OIDC_ARGOCD_SECRET` under
     `global.env`.

   <Aside type="caution">
   Push this only once both entries exist. Authentik's new pods can't start while a secret they
   read is missing (the old pods keep running until then, so nothing breaks, but nothing changes
   either).
   </Aside>

3. **Register the two apps in the blueprint.** Copy the **"Page 3"** part of
   `manifests/authentik/blueprint.yaml`: the `grafana` and `argocd` OIDC providers, their
   applications and their `admins` bindings. Replace `lab.home.example.com`.

4. **Grafana.** Add `manifests/monitoring/grafana-oidc.yaml` next to chapter 10's
   `secrets.yaml` (and to its `kustomization.yaml` if you have one). In `apps/monitoring.yaml`, add
   the Grafana `envValueFrom` and `grafana.ini` blocks from the example file. Replace
   `lab.home.example.com` everywhere.

   What the settings do:
   - `role_attribute_path`: members of `admins` become Grafana **Admin**; `role_attribute_strict`
     refuses anyone else.
   - `email_attribute_path`: Grafana needs an email for each user. If a user has none in
     Authentik, they get `<username>@lab.home.example.com`.

5. **ArgoCD.** Replace `manifests/argocd/values.yaml` with the example's (chapter 5's file plus
   the OIDC settings). It adds:
   - `configs.cm.oidc.config`: the login with Authentik, without ArgoCD's bundled Dex;
   - `configs.rbac`: `g, admins, role:admin`;
   - an ExternalSecret `argocd-oidc` for the client secret, labelled so ArgoCD reads it.

   Replace `lab.home.example.com`. Commit and push everything to `main`.

6. **Wait for ArgoCD**, then check the two logins exist:

   ```sh title="Laptop"
   kubectl get externalsecret -A | grep oidc
   curl -s https://auth.lab.home.example.com/application/o/grafana/.well-known/openid-configuration | head -c 120; echo
   curl -s https://auth.lab.home.example.com/application/o/argocd/.well-known/openid-configuration | head -c 120; echo
   ```

   The three ExternalSecrets (`authentik-oidc`, `grafana-oidc`, `argocd-oidc`) are `SecretSynced`,
   and each `curl` prints the start of a JSON document with an `issuer`.

7. **Log in.** Open `https://grafana.lab.home.example.com` and click **Sign in with Authentik**;
   then `https://argocd.lab.home.example.com` and **Log in via Authentik**. You come back logged in,
   as an administrator in both.

</Steps>

## Check it

- In Grafana, **Administration → Users** lists you, with role **Admin**.
- In ArgoCD, **User Info** shows your username and the group `admins`, and you can sync an app.
- Logging in to Grafana as `admin` with chapter 10's password still works.

## If ArgoCD logs you in but shows no apps

ArgoCD gives rights by group. If you see an empty list and "permission denied", your token has no
`admins` group: check that your user is in `admins` in Authentik, and that `configs.rbac.scopes`
is `"[groups]"`.

## Before you move on

- [ ] Grafana and ArgoCD log you in through Authentik, as an administrator.
- [ ] Their local `admin` logins still work.
- [ ] The two client secrets exist only in the secrets manager.
